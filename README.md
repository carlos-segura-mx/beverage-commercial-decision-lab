# Beverage Commercial Decision Lab

**Where does contribution come from, which outlets deserve review, and when does a discount create volume without value?**

[English](README.md) | [Español](docs/README.es.md) | [Methodology](docs/methodology.md) | [Data and assumptions](docs/data_provenance.md)

I built this independent case to examine a familiar commercial trade-off. A price discount can increase sales volume while lowering the revenue and contribution generated by a product portfolio. The project combines synthetic beverage sales, forecast evaluation and a six-product pricing simulator to make those trade-offs visible before a commercial decision.

The data are fully synthetic. The results are demonstrations of analytical logic, not sales, profitability or promotion outcomes from a real company.

## What I found

The synthetic model covers **37,440** weekly point-of-sale and product records, representing **104** weeks, **60** points of sale and six beverage products.

| Observed inside the synthetic model | Result |
| --- | ---: |
| Synthetic sell-out revenue | MXN 15.38 million |
| Synthetic contribution | MXN 8.02 million |
| Contribution margin | 52.2% |
| Order-delivery service ratio | 96.3% |
| Lowest aggregate eight-week holdout WAPE | 5.80%, eight-week moving average |

In the reference price scenario, a **10% discount on Classic Coffee** changes the six-product portfolio as follows:

| Scenario output | Change vs. unchanged prices |
| --- | ---: |
| Weekly unit volume | **+1.94%** |
| Weekly revenue | **-0.48%** |
| Weekly contribution | **-2.59%** |

The practical lesson is to evaluate volume, revenue and contribution together rather than judge a promotion from units alone. These outputs are conditional on simulated costs and price elasticities; they are not a recommended discount for a real market.

## Explore the commercial dashboard

[Open the live bilingual Streamlit app](https://beverage-commercial-decision-lab-k9yerefuyjpyvgpjke7zny.streamlit.app/).

The application now includes six connected decision views:

1. **Executive Overview:** revenue, contribution, units, weighted service, weekly trends and comparison with an equally long preceding period where available.
2. **Commercial Performance:** product and outlet rankings, channel and region comparisons, and a downloadable outlet breakdown.
3. **Service & Opportunities:** service reviews based on delivered versus ordered units, and separate reviews of low-volume outlets against same-product, same-channel peers.
4. **Forecasting:** historical model comparison, product-level WAPE and bias, and observed versus predicted quantities in the eight-week holdout.
5. **Price Simulator:** concurrent 0% to 20% discounts for six products, optional substitution assumptions, portfolio impacts and sensitivity analysis.
6. **Methodology:** assumptions, definitions and interpretation limits.

The first three views share date, channel, region and product filters. By default they display the latest **13 synthetic weeks**. The 104-week figures above refer to the full dataset. The simulator and forecast evaluation retain their own historical bases and are not silently changed by the commercial filters.

The service and commercial lists are prompts for investigation. They are not predicted stockouts, lost sales, incremental revenue or real customer opportunities.

## How the model works

The pricing engine calculates simultaneous changes for all six products:

`scenario volume = max(0, baseline volume * (1 + sum(elasticity * proportional price change)))`

Own-price elasticities are fitted to synthetic observations. Cross-price elasticities and substitution relationships are assumptions. Revenue and contribution use synthetic sell-out prices and synthetic unit costs. No causal response, real commercial benefit or optimal price has been established.

The forecasting comparison uses an eight-week historical holdout. The eight-week moving average achieved **5.80% WAPE**, while Holt-Winters delivered almost the same WAPE with a lower absolute bias in this evaluation. That result should not be treated as a permanent model ranking.

## Reproduce

```bash
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python -m pytest -q
streamlit run app.py
```

The Streamlit app reads the existing synthetic data and generated scenario inputs. To regenerate the underlying synthetic analysis, see [methodology](docs/methodology.md) and [validation](docs/validation.md) before running `python src/build_analysis.py`, because the historical script also generates analytical outputs and may write to the notebook folder.

## Scope and limitations

The revenue and contribution figures follow assumed consumer **sell-out** transactions. They do not represent distributor sell-in revenue, net profit or business performance. The historical inventory generator does not guarantee weekly stock continuity, so this dashboard does not draw replenishment conclusions from those balances.

The existing [Power BI file](powerbi/Beverage_Commercial_Decision_Lab.pbix) is preserved as a complementary historical artifact, but its relationships, measures, filters and totals have not been independently validated in this release. The application is the principal interactive deliverable. No Excel, PowerPoint or PDF simulator is claimed as a public deliverable.

The public code has no separate open-source license selected. The synthetic dataset and code are offered here for reviewing the case and its reproducibility, not as real company data.
